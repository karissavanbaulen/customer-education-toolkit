# Business case template

## Where I used this

I've used this structure twice for big asks. The first was building a docs team out of a 7-person support team writing help articles on the side. The second was buying our first LMS. Both were approved. The LMS became the platform for an academy and a certification program that certified 1,000+ users in its first month.

The lesson both times: "everyone knows docs are important" is not a business case. Leadership says yes to numbers they already track and to a plan for how the money gets spent.

## Before you write it

- Find out which KPIs your stakeholders care about right now. Use those, not yours.
- Audit what you have: people and process, how your content looks and is found, how current it is, what competitors do.
- Find an advocate early. They'll spot the holes before leadership does.
- Collect quotes. One real customer comment beats a slide of charts.

## What we need

{The ask, with a cost range. Example: "A learning management system, {€X–€Y} a year."}

## What can't happen if we don't do this

1. {Something a stakeholder wants that's blocked}
2. {Something customers are asking for that you can't give them}
3. {Something that will need extra headcount instead}

## How this helps the business grow

Write one section per team, in that team's language.

- **Sales:** {faster understanding of value, better close rates, visibility into which leads engage}
- **Customer Success:** {less time on manual training, tailored onboarding without a 1:1 call}
- **Partners:** {self-serve partner training, fewer partner managers needed}
- **Marketing / Brand:** {education content as acquisition content}
- **Support:** {deflection, savings, sessions that currently end in a ticket}
- **Our own team:** {what we can do without hiring more people}

## Expected ROI

{Your data, per team. Good sources: retention of people who used your content vs people who didn't, conversion rates, support cost per ticket, help center sessions that ended in a ticket. Mark anything you still need from another team: "Need number from {name}".}

## Risks

{Name them before someone else does.}

1. {Pricing risk, e.g. per-user overage charges}
2. {Unknowns, e.g. time to build the first course}
3. {Features you might not use}
4. {Dependencies on other projects}

## If it's about headcount, add the plan

- Hiring projections: who, when, and in what order
- How the team scales as the company grows
- How work gets split (per product squad, per topic, per content type)
- The skills each role needs
